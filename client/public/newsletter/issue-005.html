③ Worth Knowing

10 Active AI IP Court Cases You Should Know About

Anthony C dropped a full legal briefing in the chat this week triggered by Big Steele mentioning IP cases in class. This isn't abstract news — if you're building AI products, charging clients for AI-powered services, or creating content with AI tools, these cases are directly relevant to how the industry will look in 18 months. The headline signal: "Fair use decisions on AI training are in a holding pattern — no court is expected to rule on fair use in AI training until summer 2026 at the earliest."

The cases to know: OpenAI copyright litigation (SDNY) where discovery could crack open how LLMs memorize training data. UMG/WMG v. Anthropic — a $3.1 billion lawsuit filed January 2026 alleging Claude was built on torrented piracy. Disney/Universal v. Midjourney on image generation. RIAA v. Suno and UMG v. Udio both settled in late 2025 with licensing deals — Suno is launching a new model built on licensed music only, which is setting the template the rest of the industry will follow. And Thaler v. Perlmutter where SCOTUS denied cert in March 2026, closing the door on fully autonomous AI authorship but not on AI-assisted work.

The practical read: the torrenting/seeding angle on training data is the most legally dangerous exposure for any AI company right now. The settlements are writing the licensing framework that everyone will have to follow. This space is not the Wild West anymore — it's becoming regulated terrain and the group's building on top of it. Worth staying informed.
