① Main Story

The Fractional CAIO Play: How to Stack $500K a Year Without Hiring a Soul

Derrick broke down the exact math on why companies would rather pay $1K a week for someone who manages their AI team than hire staff — and why that's your opportunity right now.

A Fractional CAIO is a Chief AI Integration Officer — but part-time, across multiple clients. You're not an employee. You're the person who builds the AI-powered team infrastructure, manages the agents and automations, and keeps the whole system running. Think of it like being a COO for hire, except instead of managing people, you're managing AI workflows that do what people used to do.

Derrick laid it out Friday night after InvestFest: the people he's meeting aren't trying to hire full-time staff at $1K a week. They want someone to come in, build the system, and manage it. His math was simple — dedicate 4 hours per week per client, build their portal, manage their AI staff. Run 10 clients, you're at 40 to 50 hours a week, but you're pulling $4K to $5K per client per month. That's $40K to $50K a month. $500K to $600K a year.

Here's where people get stuck: they think they need to be a deep technical engineer to play this role. You don't. The tools exist. The gap is someone who understands how to build the system, manage the outputs, and be the human accountability layer. That's exactly what this community has been training to do. The market is waiting. The question is whether you're going to position yourself for it.

  • 4 Hours Per Week Per Client: That's the model. Build once, manage ongoing. You're not trading time for money — you're trading expertise for a monthly retainer.
  • The $1K Stack: Companies are already budgeting $1K a week for this. They'd rather pay a Fractional CAIO than deal with hiring, HR, and managing staff. That budget already exists — you just need to be the person they call.
  • 10 Clients Is the Ceiling: 10 clients at $4K-$5K per month each maxes you out at $40K-$50K monthly. That's not a fantasy number. That's a business model with math that works.

③ Worth Knowing

Lovable Is About to Train on Your Work — Here's What You Need to Know

Starting September 9, 2026, Lovable's updated privacy policy allows them to use your prompts, code, project files, and generated outputs from Free and Pro plans to train their AI models. Anthony flagged it first, then Derrick shared the full policy text for the group. The opt-out exists — go to Account Settings then Privacy — and if you opt out before September 9, your data is never used. If you opt out after, it stops going forward. Business and Enterprise plan users are covered under a separate Data Processing Agreement and this change does not affect them.

If you're building client work or anything proprietary on a Free or Pro Lovable plan, do yourself a favor and opt out before the deadline. Takes 30 seconds. The platform is still excellent — but your intellectual property is yours and you should treat it that way.

Sentry + Microsoft Clarity = Your App's Early Warning System

Derrick shared two monitoring tools every builder should have set up from day one. Sentry logs issues inside your app with enough detail for an AI agent to diagnose and fix them before users even notice. Microsoft Clarity at clarity.microsoft.com gives you heatmaps and screen recordings of actual users interacting with your app. LaSean already runs Sentry on all his builds. Big Steele confirmed Clarity is the heatmap tool he mentioned during the Junkies Admin Portal walkthrough. Both are free to start.
