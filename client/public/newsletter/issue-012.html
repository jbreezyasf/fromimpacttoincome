① Main Story

Your Website Is Built. But Can AI Find You?

Anthony's upcoming session flipped a switch for a lot of members — if AI can't find your business, you don't exist to the next wave of customers.

Here's the shift nobody told you about. People used to Google something, click a few links, and land on a website. That's changing fast. Now, more and more people ask an AI assistant for a recommendation — and the AI just answers. No links, no browsing. It either knows you or it doesn't. If your business isn't somewhere that AI can learn from, you're invisible before the conversation even starts.

Anthony put it plainly in the chat: 'Websites are no longer the starting point. They are the place people visit after AI has already decided who to recommend.' He's building a whole session around this — covering why beautiful websites aren't enough anymore, how AI decides which businesses to mention, and what you need to publish so AI can understand and trust your brand. The session is Monday June 29th, 6-7:30pm ET. Don't sleep on it.

The mistake most people make is thinking SEO is still the whole game. It's not. Traditional SEO got you ranked on Google. AIO — AI Optimization — is about making sure the models themselves have enough structured, credible, consistent information about you that when someone asks Claude or ChatGPT for a recommendation in your niche, your name comes up. Two completely different strategies. Most businesses haven't started the second one yet.

  • AI answers before your site loads: When someone asks an AI tool who to hire or what to buy, it responds from its training and retrieval — not from browsing your homepage. Your site matters less if the model never learned you exist.
  • AIO is not the same as SEO: Search engine optimization got you ranked in Google results. AI Optimization is about being cited, referenced, and recommended by AI systems. You need both now, and most people are only doing one.
  • Publish so AI can understand you: Structured content, consistent messaging, clear niche signals — these are what help AI systems recognize and trust your business. Vague branding and buried information won't cut it.
💼 How does this apply to your business?

Say you're building voice agents for real estate agents. If a potential client asks Claude 'who builds AI voice agents for real estate,' you want to be the name that comes up. That only happens if you've published content, gotten referenced in enough places, and established enough signal that the model connects your name to that niche. This isn't a future problem. It's a right now problem.

④ Hot Topic
🔥 From the Group Chat

Tool Fluency vs. Judgment — Anthony Put It in the Chat and We Need to Talk About It

Anthony dropped a post mid-week that didn't get as much reaction as it deserved, but it's probably the most important thing said in the group all week. The debate: is being fast with AI tools actually valuable if your judgment is bad?

This matters for everyone building in this space — whether you're hiring, training clients, or positioning yourself as an AI expert. The person who catches the model's mistake before it reaches a client is worth ten times the person who just prompts fast. As we build more autonomous systems, judgment is the moat. Speed is table stakes.

Anthony
"The scary employee isn't the one who can't use AI. It's the one who uses it confidently and can't tell when it's wrong. Speed doesn't fix judgment. It amplifies whatever judgment is already there."
Anthony
"Tool fluency is cheap now. Judgment is the thing you're actually paying for."
Anthony
"We've watched teams hand the keys to whoever was fastest with the tool, and then spend three weeks unwinding plausible-looking output that was quietly wrong."
