① Main Story

The 3-Day Blueprint Wrapped — And the Room Was Different by Day 3

Britt, Rodney, and LaSean each took a night and delivered — here's what actually landed.

The 3-Day Blueprint to Your First $5K/Month ran June 2 through June 4, with VIP sessions capping each night. The format was simple: one instructor per night, one focused skill set, and homework you were expected to show up with the next day. Day 1 was Britt. Day 2 brought in Rodney. LaSean closed it out on Day 3. Each session built on the one before it, which is exactly how it was designed.

By the end of Day 2, Natasha was up past midnight smoke-testing her app while working through session notes at the same time. LaRonz dropped his avatar homework in the chat before Day 2 even started. Karen called Day 2 a game-changer. And when LaSean wrapped Day 3, Dr. Regina Turner, Karen Foster, Sherry Carter, Wendy Grace, and Towana Garrett all came through the chat with the same energy — he pulled the pieces together in a way that made the whole system click.

Here's the thing people miss about a 3-day format like this: Day 1 alone won't move you. Day 2 alone won't either. The compounding is the point. If you caught the replays out of order or skipped a night, you felt it. The members who stayed locked in all three days walked away with a complete picture. The ones who dipped in and out got fragments. That's not a knock — it's just how stacking works.

  • Demo before dollars: Derrick gave James direct feedback after his live Facebook demo of Sister Clark: he went to the price before showing what the product actually does. Derrick's note was clean — price first forces people to tune out or only attract folks who think it's cheap. Drive the value with the demo first, then name the number.
  • Show your homework: LaRonz and Dr. Regina both dropped their avatar builds in the chat during the event. That's not just accountability — it signals to everyone else that the work is doable. When people see peers completing assignments, momentum builds across the whole group.
  • VIP is included: Multiple members asked whether the VIP room required a separate payment. Big Steele and Britt both confirmed: if the call shows up in your portal, you have access. Trust Robo.
💼 How does this apply to your business?

If you're thinking about offering a 3-day workshop or training to a client's audience, this week was a live masterclass in how to structure it. Each day builds on the last, homework creates accountability between sessions, and the compounding effect does the selling for you by Day 3. That model works whether you're teaching realtors to use AI, helping a nonprofit staff get efficient, or running a paid cohort of your own.

③ Worth Knowing

Anthropic Filed a Confidential S-1. Here's Why That Matters to Builders.

On June 1, Anthropic officially submitted a draft registration statement on Form S-1 to the SEC — the first step toward going public. They were clear that no shares or price have been set yet, and filing confidentially just means the SEC reviews it before it becomes public. It is not a guaranteed IPO. It is an option to go public once the review is complete and market conditions line up. Derrick dropped the official announcement straight from Anthropic in the chat.

For this group specifically, this matters for two reasons. First, Claude is our primary build tool — knowing that Anthropic is heading toward a public offering means more institutional scrutiny, more pressure to maintain and grow their product, and likely more competition for them to stay ahead of. That's generally good for users. Second, and more immediately: Derrick also noted that Claude's new habit of telling people to take breaks is a legal precaution tied to an active class action. Going public means more of that kind of institutional caution, not less. Build accordingly.

⑤ Quick Hits
01
Big Steele's Three-Persona Prompt for Claude

When Claude starts giving generic or shallow responses, Big Steele dropped a prompt that snaps it back into focus: have it analyze your product from three angles — the person who knows nothing about AI, the person who tried to build and quit, and the person with ideas but poor execution. Thorough, real-logic analysis across all three. He said it got Claude back in line immediately. Save it.

02
Animate Your Book Characters with Kling or Google Flow

Carrien is turning her urban memoir into a mini series by animating characters she already created. Laura Covile's answer was direct: use ChatGPT to generate the image, then run it through Kling or Google Flow to animate it. That's a complete content-to-promo pipeline for any author or creator with existing visual assets.

03
Build a Zoom Chat Agent with n8n and the Zoom REST API

Kre was grinding for six hours trying to get n8n to fire messages inside a Zoom meeting. Big Steele pointed out the missing piece: you need the Zoom REST API connected via an HTTP node in n8n, and you need n8n-mcp attached to Claude so it can actually build and test the workflow. Without n8n-mcp, Claude is guessing. With it, Claude can make the workflow directly.

04
PostGrid for Physical Postcards at $0.86 a Piece

The group was building out a Google Maps scraping play using Claude Code and the Apify API to pull business data. Juanita dropped PostGrid as the final piece — customized physical postcards starting at about 86 cents per piece. That closes the loop from AI-scraped lead to physical mail in someone's hands. The tech stack for outreach just got analog again.
