① Main Story

HotRod Built a Lead Machine and Gave It Away

The Local Prospector agent finds small businesses with weak websites, audits them automatically, and hands you qualified leads with a full dossier — and HotRod shared the whole thing with the group.

HotRod P dropped a Claude-based agent into the chat Monday night called the Local Prospector. Here is what it actually does: it searches your area for local businesses, goes and reads every page of their website, scores each one against an evidence-based rubric, and returns a ranked list of leads. Not just names — a full breakdown of every weakness, tied to the exact page where the problem lives. The goal is precision. It would rather give you five airtight leads than ten shaky ones. Once you set it up, it runs on a weekly schedule.

The group immediately started stress-testing it. Britt was up past 2am and came back the next day with a full workflow: she took HotRod's plugin, had Claude repurpose it for her specific industry, validated what services were actually in demand with research first, then used the results to identify additional plugins she should build — including a curriculum builder. She also somehow ended up doing a YouTube audit and updating her channel in the process. That is a week's worth of work that happened in one sitting.

Here is where people get this wrong: they see a tool like this and think about using it one time. The real move is what Britt flagged — this can be repurposed as a skill that feeds into a larger system. A prospecting agent is the top of a funnel. The businesses it finds still need someone to reach out, run a discovery call, and close. The agent handles the research. You handle the relationship. Know the difference.

  • Evidence-Based Scoring: Every weakness the agent finds is tied back to the specific page it came from. You show up to a prospect already knowing exactly what's broken and where.
  • Repurpose the Core Logic: Britt took HotRod's plugin and retargeted it for her industry in one session. The underlying logic — find, audit, score, report — works across almost any vertical.
  • Set It and Forget It: Once configured, it runs weekly. You are not doing manual research anymore. Your job becomes reviewing the output and making moves.
💼 How does this apply to your business?

Say you do website builds and AI integrations for local service businesses — salons, contractors, restaurants. Instead of cold calling blind, you deploy this agent targeting your city. It comes back Sunday night with five businesses whose sites are clearly hurting them: no mobile optimization, no contact form, no booking system. Monday morning you reach out already holding the receipts. That is the difference between a cold call and a warm opener.

③ Worth Knowing

ChatGPT Just Shipped Scheduled Tasks — and It Is More Useful Than It Sounds

Cheryl flagged this Wednesday: OpenAI added a proper Scheduled Tasks page inside ChatGPT. You can now set up recurring tasks, reminders, research briefings, and monitoring tasks directly in the app. The monitoring piece is the one to pay attention to — you can tell ChatGPT to watch for specific changes and only notify you when something meaningful happens. That is not just a calendar feature. That is the beginning of an async agent layer built into a tool most of your clients already have open in their browser.

Practical angle: if you are building for clients who are not technical, this is something you can set up for them in minutes. A weekly competitive research briefing, a Monday morning summary of industry news, a task reminder for their team — none of it requires API access or a custom build. It is in the ChatGPT interface right now. Go explore it before your next client call so you can speak to it confidently.

🔥 From the Group Chat

Anthropic Hit With a Class Action — and the Chat Had Thoughts

Big Steele dropped the news Tuesday that a class action lawsuit is being introduced against Anthropic. The thread that followed went from legal talk to a real conversation about platform dependency and what builders should actually be doing to protect themselves.

This is not just about Anthropic. Every builder in this group who relies on a single API provider for their core product needs an exit strategy. Rate limits, usage caps, pricing changes — the platforms will always prioritize their own growth over your business continuity. The question is not if they change the rules on you. It is when. Build like you know that.
