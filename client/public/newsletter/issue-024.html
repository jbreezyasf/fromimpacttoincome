① Main Story

Instinct Hit the Chat Like a Fire Alarm at 1AM — Here's What It Actually Does

A new agentic tool called Instinct went from unknown to every DM in the group inside of 24 hours, and members are already pulling leads, scrubbing personal data, and running GitHub audits with it.

Instinct is an AI agent — think of it as a tireless assistant that can browse the web, collect information, take actions on your behalf, and report back. It's invite-only right now and still free. You give it a goal, not just a question, and it goes out and tries to accomplish it. That's the big difference between this and a regular chatbot. You're not prompting it back and forth — you're delegating.

Cheryl dropped the first invite link on Wednesday night and it spread fast. By Thursday the group was comparing notes. Dom said it had already found him $1,500. Cheryl said she was up $10K. Juanita had it build a referral partner list — complete with usernames and niches — for her apps. Anthony ran a full personal data privacy audit with a prompt so thorough it took two messages to paste. Nathan used it to research his Skool community and draft custom DMs for each member. Cheryl connected her GitHub and had it audit which repos were accidentally public.

The misconception to dodge: Instinct is not magic, it's leverage. The members getting real results were the ones who came in with a clear outcome — leads for a specific niche, a 30/90/180-day brand roadmap, a privacy sweep. Juanita put it best: she's using it hard while the free credits last, but she's also rebuilding Hermes locally because she does not want her data sitting on someone else's cloud. Nathan flagged the same concern about data ownership. The tool is powerful. Know what you're handing it.

  • Come with an outcome, not a prompt: Anthony asked Instinct to build an empire around his personal brand site and got a full audit with a 30, 90, and 12-month roadmap. Nathan got a weekly scoreboard. Vague inputs get vague outputs.
  • Use ChatGPT to generate your Instinct prompts: Cheryl told ChatGPT to give her 5 high-value, revenue-generating instructions for Instinct based on everything Chat already knows about her work. She said the result was jaw-dropping. Borrow that play.
  • Watch your data — local is the long game: Juanita plans to rebuild her Hermes agent locally this weekend and replace Instinct once it's right. If you already set up OpenClaw or Hermes in the early cohort sessions, those skills are suddenly very relevant again.

② Also This Week

AI Case Law Is Here — Big Steele Said Start Thinking About Private Infrastructure

Big Steele dropped a link Wednesday afternoon with two words: 'Here comes AI case law.' A legal ruling tied to AI-generated work product is starting to move through the system, and it's the kind of thing that will reshape how businesses think about what they build with AI tools and who owns it.

Big's take was direct and practical: if you need IP protection, attorney-client privilege, or any kind of legal cover around your work product, the custom route is the only route. That means knowing how to run open-source LLMs on private servers, build custom RAGs, and keep your data off shared cloud infrastructure. Juanita reminded the group that anyone who came through the October-December 2025 cohort already has those skills — Docker, VS Code, Cursor, Ollama — they just need to dust them off.

Natasha connected this to something she brought back from a CMS call: NIST is actively working on AI testing and evaluation frameworks. The acronym is TEVV — Testing, Evaluation, Validation, and Verification. Worth knowing the name before it becomes a compliance requirement.

④ Hot Topic
🔥 From the Group Chat

Instinct vs. Hermes vs. OpenClaw — Where Should Your Agent Actually Live?

Once the Instinct invites spread and people started getting results, a real question surfaced: is this tool better than what we already built, or are we just distracted by something shiny and free?

This is the core tension in the group right now. Instinct is fast, capable, and costs nothing today — but your data is on their servers and nobody knows when the billing starts. Hermes and OpenClaw sit on your machine, your rules, your knowledge base. The members who came through the early workshops have the skills to run local agents. The question is whether they use them. This debate is going to matter more as AI regulation tightens and the token cost explosion Derrick predicted gets closer.

Juanita
"Hermes and Instinct are like sister cousins. If you guys already have your agents, go back, give it the guardrails, give it the brain. This is what Instinct is, what OpenClaw and Hermes are. Hermes just creates its own skills so I don't have to go in and create skills for it. If it doesn't know something, it'll start creating a skill for it — it has its own self-healing loop."
Nathan
"I think they're similar, just the difference is where ownership of the data is. The OpenClaw/Hermes is more you have to manage the system, while Instinct has their cloud that can handle it. I do like how Instinct can do and read more of the research better."
Cheryl Tibbs
"Everybody is pushing local setup. I say all this to say: what are your thoughts on Hermes? With what's going on with them talking about slowing down AI, and Andrew Yang saying the ChatGPT agent that supposedly hacked into Hugging Face hit some other websites — things are about to get crazy."
