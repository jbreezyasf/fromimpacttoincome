① Main Story

Tequila Almost Gave Away the Keys to Her House — Here's What Saved Her

A live moment in the chat turned into the most important security lesson of the week: there is a big difference between sharing your app and sharing your project.

When you build on a platform like Lovable, you have two very different things: the app itself — the thing users interact with — and the project, which is the codebase, the backend, the blueprints. Sharing a link to your app so someone can test it is totally fine. Granting someone access to your project is handing them the keys. Most new builders do not know the difference until something almost goes wrong.

Tequila was trying to get feedback on her pilot — a legit thing to do. She dropped a preview link in the chat and mentioned she was about to grant access to a member who requested it. LaSean Pickens caught it immediately: 'Why are you giving someone access to your project? As in your codebase?' He followed up fast: 'Oh I just looked, no, don't do that.' Althea Cooper jumped in with the practical fix — she had already set up testers on the backend of her own Lovable project and invited five founding testers that way. Tequila adjusted, created a viewable tour link instead, and protected her work.

This is where builders get tripped up all the time. You want feedback, you want eyes on your thing, and the fastest path feels like just sharing a link. But preview links inside Lovable behave differently depending on whether your project is published, and access requests from other Lovable users go straight to your codebase — not just a view of the front end. The right move: build a public-facing demo or tour page, invite beta testers as users inside the app itself, and never approve a codebase access request unless you know exactly who is on the other end and why.

  • Project Access vs. App Access: Granting project access in Lovable lets someone see and potentially edit your codebase. Sharing a published or preview link lets them see the app experience. Know which one you are doing before you click confirm.
  • Build a Beta Tester Flow Inside Your App: Create user accounts on the backend of your app and invite testers through those. Althea had already done this with five founding testers. Ask Lovable to build the admin side if you do not have it yet.
  • Connect to GitHub Before You Share Anything: Althea asked Tequila if her Lovable project was connected to GitHub. It was not. Connecting early gives you version control and a backup before anyone else gets near your work.

③ Worth Knowing

If You Are Running PHP, Stop Reading and Go Update Right Now

Big Steele dropped a critical security alert Wednesday morning that applies to anyone hosting their own backend on PHP. Two vulnerabilities — CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 — are rated 9.2 out of 10 on the CVSS v4.0 severity scale. That is as serious as it gets. Both allow unauthenticated remote code execution, meaning someone who has never touched your system could run code on it from anywhere. The fixed versions are PHP 8.2.35+, 8.3.36+, 8.4.27+, and 8.5.12+. If you are on 8.1 or older, you need to upgrade branches entirely.

The part most people skip: patching does not clean up a compromise that already happened. After you update, restart your PHP workers, verify the server is running the fixed build, and then actually review your logs and files for web shells, backdoors, or anything that looks off. If you cannot patch right now, restrict access or take the service offline. There is no partial fix here.
