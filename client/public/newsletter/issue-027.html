⑤ Quick Hits
01
Prompt hack for realistic AI images — name the flaw, not the vibe

Kre dropped a full breakdown this week: vague words like 'beautiful' and 'realistic' push your image toward that generic plasticky AI look. Real images come from naming specific details — visible pores, peach fuzz catching the light, stray hairs, damp flyaways, subtle under-eye bags. Use GPT or Claude to generate a list of specific imperfections based on the person you are trying to create, then put those in the prompt. The bad example is 'gorgeous woman, realistic, amazing quality.' The good example describes the lip texture, the flushed cheeks, the phone camera angle, and blocks the bad outputs explicitly: no smoothing, no plastic skin.

02
Recover your best video prompts before you lose them

Anthony dropped a prompt you can run in whatever tool you have been generating videos with: ask it to create a PDF of every prompt from the past two weeks that directly preceded a video generation, with the exact wording, date, timestamp, and sequential number. Only prompts that resulted in an actual video. Chronological order. This is your creative asset library — stop letting those prompts disappear into scroll history.

03
Instinct as a project manager is underrated

Nathan and Althea both spoke to this independently. Nathan has Instinct tracking his weekly plan, following up on Fridays, and maintaining a dashboard of what is in each stage — it even helped him plan a surprise cruise trip. Althea had it build a 30-day social media calendar with captions, image descriptions, and pinned comments across three platforms, plus a Google Sheets dashboard with tabs for tasks, prospects, and ideas for each software launch. The accountability angle is real. It will get on your last nerve when you get sidetracked, which is the point.
