① Main Story

Jay Walked Into a DoD Meeting With One Product and Left With a Contract Path for Two

A GovCon accountability tool built inside AI Junkies just got validated by a Director at a Department of Defense Small Business office — and the government asked for more.

Jay built a product focused on GovCon accountability — helping government contractors stay on track and answerable to their commitments. He set up a meeting with a Director from a DoD Small Business office expecting honest feedback. What he got instead was excitement. The government loved what he built. Then things got even better.

During that same meeting, the Director said their office could use a similar tool on their end — to track contractor performance from the government side. Jay came in with one product pitch and left with validation for two separate tools, one for contractors and one for the agencies overseeing them. The group lit up when he posted about it. Britt immediately handed him a full production stack: HeyGen or Google Veo for animation, ChatGPT to clean up product images, CapCut for editing. Big Steele pointed him to Loom for the 2-3 minute demo video the DoD requested. Tequila reminded him HeyGen has a built-in product demo feature.

Here's where people get it wrong with government work: they think the barrier is the tech. It is not. Jay's barrier was getting in the room. He got in the room because he built something real that solved a real problem. The demo video they asked for is not the hard part — that is just execution. The hard part was showing up with something worth asking for a demo of. He already cleared the hard part.

  • Build the real thing first: Jay did not pitch a concept or a deck. He built an actual product around GovCon accountability. That is what got the meeting and that is what got the second ask.
  • The demo video is just documentation: When the government asks for a 2-3 minute demo, that is not a creative challenge — it is a documentation task. Loom, HeyGen, CapCut. The group had Jay covered in under ten minutes.
  • One product validated can open the door to a second contract: The DoD did not just approve what Jay built — they saw a version of it they wanted for themselves. Pay attention. That is how government relationships scale.
💼 How does this apply to your business?

If you are sitting on a niche tool you built for one side of an industry, ask yourself who else in that same transaction needs the same visibility. Jay built for contractors. The agency regulating them wanted the same thing. That is not a coincidence — that is product-market fit hiding in plain sight.
