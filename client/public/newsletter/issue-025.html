① Main Story

Cheryl Just Opened a Funding Lane Nobody Else Is Talking About

A vendor-pairing model for AI builders without business tenure could be the most practical monetization move this group has seen all year.

When Cheryl Tibbs dropped a voice note about how to get paid as a software vendor and developer, it kicked off something bigger than a tip. The core idea: lenders and institutions often require a business to have years of operating history before they can be approved as a vendor or receive funding. Most new AI builders and vibe coders don't have that yet — but some people in this group do.

Cheryl's AI company is three years old. Towana's has been established four years. Natasha's has been registered with the state for five. That tenure matters to lenders. Cheryl floated the idea of building a partner directory — tenured businesses that newer builders can co-vendor under for a percentage, somewhere between 10 and 20 percent. Derrick co-signed it immediately: 'This is dope. I was about to add the funding platform in here for this exact reason.' Cheryl responded: 'Yes, I'm working on it to go out this week. We should collab.'

The mistake most new builders make is thinking they have to wait until they're 'established' to access real business funding. That's backwards. The infrastructure might already exist inside this group. The gap isn't skill — it's tenure on paper. And if you can plug into someone who already has that, you skip years of waiting.

  • Tenure Is the Unlock: Lenders care how long your business has been open. Three to five years often qualifies you as a vendor or for equipment/software financing. If you don't have it, partnering with someone who does is a real move.
  • The Co-Vendor Model: Cheryl is building a directory of tenured businesses willing to co-sign newer developers as vendors for a percentage fee. This is not theoretical — she said it's going out this week and Derrick is collaborating on it.
  • Know Your Business Status: Dr. Regina confirmed she's already set up properly to be funded. Do you know your own status? If your business has been registered for a few years, you may already be sitting on leverage you haven't used.

④ Hot Topic
🔥 From the Group Chat

Britt's Boss Asked Her to Build a Marketing GPT — The Group Said Charge for It

Britt came to the chat Friday night with a real dilemma: her employer asked her to build a custom marketing GPT and migrate existing GPTs to plugins — work that is clearly outside her job description and hourly rate. She asked what everyone would do.

This conversation hit differently because it's not just Britt's situation. It's every person in this group who is leveling up their skills while still working a day job. The moment your employer figures out you can do things they'd otherwise pay consultants tens of thousands of dollars for, scope creep starts. Knowing how to name it — Scope of Work, project rate, out of job description — is as important as knowing how to build it.

LaSean Pickens
"Baby we're not doing anything for free. That's out of your job scope. They would've gladly paid someone else top dollar for it so make sure you're compensated as well. Don't let your skills be used at someone else's convenience. You put in the time, effort, and attention to master your craft. You're a professional and this is your business, treat it as such. Your work isn't a favor."
