① Main Story

A Claude Certified Partner Got Banned. Here's What You Need to Do Before It Happens to You.

Cheryl Tibbs shared a wake-up call this week — a friend who built her whole business in Claude, hosted 14 build-a-thons, got banned with no explanation the next morning.

Getting banned from Claude means losing access to your projects, your custom instructions, your entire workflow — overnight, with no warning and often no clear reason given. Anthropic doesn't usually explain bans. You can appeal, but from what folks in the group are hearing, appeals are a long shot. If your business lives inside claude.ai and that account disappears, you have a problem.

Cheryl didn't just share the bad news — she shared the fix. She ran a full audit using Claude Fable 5 and came back with two ready-to-use prompts: one to lower your ban risk by checking your workflows against Anthropic's usage policies, and one to build a full business continuity runbook so your operations survive even if the worst happens. The group ate it up — 14 reactions on the first drop, 11 on the second. Anthony jumped in and confirmed he uses Fable the same way to test builds and catch redundancies.

Here's where most people are sleeping: they've built everything inside the consumer claude.ai interface. Your chat history, your project instructions, your custom prompts — none of that is automatically backed up anywhere you control. Cheryl's audit also flagged the quiet ban vectors people never think about: failed payments, account sharing with a VA, and high-volume automated content generation that looks like spam to Anthropic's systems. The fix isn't complicated, but you have to do it before you need it.

  • Separate your rails: Your consumer claude.ai account and your Anthropic Console API org are two different things. Critical business work should live under a Console org with a business card on billing, a second admin email, and named API keys per workload — not on a personal login.
  • Your prompts should live in files you control: If your system prompts, CLAUDE.md files, and skills only exist inside chat history, one ban wipes them. Back everything up daily to iCloud, Google Drive, or an external drive with dated snapshots.
  • Audit before you assume you're clean: Cheryl ran the audit and came back 80% safe — and she's someone who lives and breathes this. Run the ban-resistance prompt she dropped in the chat with Fable 5 selected. Find out where you actually stand.
💼 How does this apply to your business?

If you're a consultant, coach, or agency owner running client deliverables through Claude — proposals, SOPs, content pipelines, voice agent logic — and your account goes down on a Tuesday morning, what happens to your clients? That's not hypothetical anymore. Build the runbook now, not after it happens.

② Also This Week

Fable 5 Is Here and It's Expensive, Powerful, and Already Smarter Than How Most People Are Using It

Claude Fable 5 landed this week and the group went deep on it fast. The pricing hit people first — $10 per million tokens on input, $50 per million on output. Big Steele joked he'd need to build a whole product just to cover the API credits. But once people got past sticker shock, the real conversation started: what's this thing actually good for, and how do you not burn through tokens doing it?

Big Steele laid out the practical breakdown: use 'high' effort setting, not 'xhigh' or 'max' — those are token furnaces with worse outputs. For anything token-heavy like computer use or codebase analysis, run it on another model and bring the results back to Fable. Cheryl went further and used it to audit all 30+ of her Claude skills in batches — finding trigger collisions between skills, an API key sitting in plaintext inside a skill file, stale hardcoded data, and duplicate skills competing with each other. The skill audit prompt she shared is the most immediately actionable thing that dropped in the group this week.

Big Steele also flagged something worth knowing: Fable 5 has broad safeguards right now that will auto-switch you to Opus 4.8 mid-conversation if it flags your task — even for routine coding or cybersecurity work. It tells you when it does it. Anthropic says they're refining the safeguards. Just know it can happen and don't be surprised when it does.
