① Main Story

Big Built an Audit Tool That Tells You Exactly Where Your App Stands

The Big Steele AI Audit grades your codebase on AI readiness, autonomy, and launch readiness — and it became the entry ticket to Tuesday's session.

If you want to know whether your app is actually built for AI or just AI-flavored, the Big Steele AI Audit will tell you fast. You run a single terminal command — npx @bigsteele/ai-audit --repo . — inside your project folder, and it reads your files, never touches your .env secrets, never runs your code, and spits out two files: a human-readable report and a JSON. The report gives you a score out of 100, a grade, and a gate result. Gate: PASS means you hit at least Level 1 Automated. The bar the strong platforms hit is Level 3 Agentic.

Big and LaSean didn't stop there. By Monday night they shipped the upgraded version — npx @bigsteele/the-big-sean — which LaSean described simply as 'on steroids.' It combines AI Readiness, Launch Readiness, and Autonomy into one scan, spawns subagents, uses more tokens, and writes a detailed report you can upload directly to bigsteele.com/scan. Malik had Claude running all morning fixing what the audit found — Claude told him straight up it would take nine hours and kept running. Scores ranged from 26 to 75 across the group, with members grinding through the holiday weekend to move the needle before Tuesday.

Here is where people got stuck: most members ran the audit to get into the room, not to act on what it said. Anthony called it out directly Wednesday — he asked who was actually implementing the audit recommendations versus who just did the assignment to avoid getting locked out. The audit is an architecture-readiness score, not a pass/fail on whether your app works. A score of 31 with Gate: PASS means your app runs but is not built to operate autonomously at scale. The recommendations — adding APIs, agent tools, MCP servers, autonomous loops, retrieval layers — are the actual work. The score is just the starting line.

  • Two tools, one job: The original ai-audit gives you a quick score. The Big Sean is the full scan — it includes everything from the first tool plus deeper autonomy analysis. Run the Big Sean. That is the one that matters.
  • Score does not mean broken: A low score is not a statement that your app is unsafe or non-functional. It means your architecture does not yet support autonomous, agent-driven operation. Lovable projects need the prompt version — the npx command does not work there.
  • Gate: PASS is the floor, not the ceiling: Passing the gate gets you in the room. Level 3 Agentic is where you want to be if you are building something you plan to sell or hand off. Nathan said it cleanly: autonomous apps have enterprise value because they do not require your constant time.

LaSean Built a Fully Autonomous Content Engine and It Generates Photos That Actually Look Like Him

LaSean shipped his autonomous content engine this week — not a template, not a scheduler, a full system. It handles branding posts, subtle ads, automatic giveaways, auto-replies, and a video production layer that treats every video like a Hollywood production. It gives him exact settings, tone, script, layout, and B-roll direction for every piece of content. The goal is ten posts a day with a photo attached plus two to three reels, all pointing back to his portfolio of products: creditsystem.io, sitelaunch.io, collapporate.io, fadehouse.app, lashtech.app, and craneop.net.

The photo engine is the piece that stopped the chat. He shared an image generated with zero in-depth prompting — just 'give me a picture of me at my desk' — and it looked like him. The reason it works is that he built a custom back-end system with a pass/fail layer that evaluates images before outputting them. It pulls from a database trained on his likeness instead of relying on chat memory. Juanita asked which image generator he was using. His answer: he built his own pipeline around ChatGPT's image output, engineered specifically so he does not have to reprompt every session.

LaSean was also honest about where he stopped. He intentionally held off on AI video generation for now. His reasoning: he does not want to start spending on a tool he might abandon in a week. That discipline — knowing when not to build — is worth noting. The engine he finished is one he will actually run.

GitHub Is Your Timeout Insurance

02
Markdown Files Are Memory for Your Codebase

Anthony dropped a full breakdown: your .md files — README.md, CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, CHANGELOG.md — are how you stop explaining your project from scratch every session. Code tells AI what exists. Markdown tells AI what you meant. Cheryl added that she uses a State.md specifically so Claude's memory persona persists between sessions.

03
Co-Building Best Practice From Kre

Michelle asked how to build with a partner without stepping on each other. Kre's answer: map the full build plan first — database, flows, features, integrations — then assign ownership by stage, not just front end versus back end. Use separate GitHub branches, communicate every change, and merge in stages. The build plan is the key; without it, partners overwrite each other's work.

04
APEX Accelerators: Free Gov Contracting Help

Jay shared a resource that flew under the radar: APEX Accelerators are DoD-funded counselors who help small businesses break into government contracting at zero cost. They cover SAM.gov registration, certification guidance for 8(a) and HUBZone programs, finding real bid opportunities, and staying compliant. No revenue minimum, no experience required. Start at apexaccelerators.us.
